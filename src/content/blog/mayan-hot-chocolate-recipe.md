## What Is Mayan Hot Chocolate?

The Maya didn't drink hot chocolate the way we think of it today. There was no sugar, no milk, no marshmallows. The original *xocolātl* was a cold, frothy, intensely bitter drink made from ground cacao, water, chili, and spices — consumed by warriors and priests before ceremonies.

This recipe honors that tradition while making it enjoyable as a modern warm drink.

## Ingredients

- 2 tablespoons raw cacao powder (or finely ground cacao nibs)
- 1 cup water or oat milk
- 1 small dried chili (árbol or ancho), or ¼ tsp cayenne
- ½ teaspoon cinnamon
- Pinch of sea salt
- 1 teaspoon Jimerito honey (or to taste)
- Optional: ¼ tsp vanilla extract

## Instructions

1. Warm the water or oat milk in a small saucepan over medium heat — do not boil.
2. Whisk in the cacao powder until fully dissolved.
3. Add the chili, cinnamon, and salt. Simmer 3 minutes.
4. Remove chili. Whisk vigorously to create a light froth.
5. Pour into a cup and sweeten with honey to taste.

The Maya prized the froth above everything else — the more froth, the better the drink.

## Why Cacao Nibs Work Better

Ground cacao nibs give you a more complex, less processed flavor than commercial cacao powder. They contain the full fat (cacao butter) which creates a richer, silkier texture.
