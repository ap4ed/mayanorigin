## Who Was Balam?

In the Mayan language, *balam* simply means jaguar. But the jaguar wasn't just an animal to the Maya — it was a god, a spirit guide, and a symbol of supreme power.

The jaguar ruled the night, the underworld, and the hidden forces of the earth. Mayan kings wore jaguar pelts and took jaguar names. Warriors called on Balam's spirit before battle. Shamans believed the jaguar could move between the world of the living and the world of the dead.

## The Jaguar in Mayan Art

Look at almost any major Mayan archaeological site — Copán, Tikal, Palenque — and you'll find the jaguar everywhere. On temples, on thrones, on ceremonial vessels used to prepare cacao drinks.

The Copán ruins in western Honduras, where our coffee is grown, contain some of the finest jaguar imagery in the entire Mayan world. The Jaguar Stairway at Copán is considered one of the greatest works of pre-Columbian art ever created.
