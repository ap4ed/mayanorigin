## Two Things Copán Is Famous For

Most people know Copán for its ruins — the ancient Mayan city that UNESCO declared a World Heritage Site in 1980. The hieroglyphic stairway at Copán contains more ancient Mayan text than any other monument ever discovered.

Fewer people know that the mountains surrounding those ruins grow some of the finest arabica coffee in Central America.

That's not a coincidence.

## Why the Mountains of Copán Grow Great Coffee

Coffee quality is determined more by geography than by anything else. The Copán highlands sit between 1,300 and 1,700 meters above sea level. At that altitude:

- **Temperatures are cooler** — coffee cherries develop more slowly, building more complex sugars
- **Volcanic soil is rich** — the same geology that sustained the Maya civilization feeds the coffee trees
- **Dry season is distinct** — a clear wet/dry cycle produces consistent, well-defined harvests

The result: dense, complex beans with bright acidity and deep sweetness.

## The Mayan Connection

The ancient Maya who built the temples at Copán also cultivated cacao in these same valleys. They traded it across Mesoamerica. They made ceremonial drinks from it. They placed cacao pods on their altars.

Coffee didn't exist in the Maya world — it arrived centuries later. But the same soil, the same altitude, the same human care for the land that made Copán's cacao legendary is what makes its coffee exceptional today.
