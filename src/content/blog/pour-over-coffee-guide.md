## Why Pour Over Rewards Good Coffee

Pour over is not the easiest way to brew coffee. It is, however, the most honest.

There's no pressure to compensate for a weak roast, no full-immersion to disguise off flavors, no milk foam to cover anything up. Pour over extracts coffee slowly, evenly, and transparently, and every quality in the bean (good or bad) shows up directly in the cup.

That's exactly why it's the best method for a high-quality light roast. The bright acidity, floral aromatics, and natural fruit sweetness that make a light roast special are fully preserved by pour over's clean extraction. A paper filter removes sediment and oils that would muddy those delicate notes, leaving a cup that's clear, vivid, and complex.

## What You Need

Pour over can be done with simple or expensive equipment. Start simple.

**Essential:**
- Pour over dripper (V60, Chemex, Kalita Wave, all work with minor differences)
- Paper filters (matched to your dripper)
- Gooseneck kettle (the narrow spout gives you pour control that a regular kettle can't)
- Kitchen scale
- Timer
- A mug or carafe underneath

**Optional but helpful:**
- Variable-temperature kettle (saves guessing)
- Burr grinder (strongly recommended, blade grinders produce uneven extractions)

The gooseneck kettle is the one piece of equipment that actually matters for pour over. The thin spout lets you pour slowly and precisely, which is the whole technique.

## The Right Ratio: Coffee to Water

The standard pour over ratio is **1:15 coffee to water by weight.**

For every 1 gram of coffee, use 15 grams (ml) of water. Here's how that looks in practice:

| Cup Size | Coffee | Water |
|---|---|---|
| Single (250ml) | 17g | 250ml |
| Large (350ml) | 23g | 350ml |
| Full Chemex (600ml) | 40g | 600ml |

Want a more intense cup? Go to 1:14. Prefer something lighter? Try 1:16. The ratio is a starting point. Dial it to your taste.

Do not eyeball this. A scale that cost $10 will improve your pour over more than a $100 grinder upgrade will. Consistency is the whole game.

## Grind Size: Medium-Fine

Pour over needs a **medium-fine grind**, finer than drip, coarser than espresso. For a complete breakdown of grind sizes across all brew methods, see our [coffee grind size guide](/blog/coffee-grind-size-guide/).

Think table salt. A particle size where you can see individual grains but they feel smooth, not gritty, between your fingers. On most grinders, this is roughly in the middle of the range, slightly toward the fine end.

Why does grind matter so much? Because pour over is a flow-through method: water passes through the grounds by gravity. Grind determines how long the water is in contact with the coffee:
- Too fine: water flows slowly, over-extracts, tastes bitter
- Too coarse: water rushes through, under-extracts, tastes sour and weak
- Just right: steady flow, balanced extraction, clean and sweet

If your pour over drains in under 2 minutes, grind finer. If it takes over 4 minutes, grind coarser. Aim for 2:30–3:30 total brew time.

## Water Temperature

The ideal range for pour over is **90–96°C / 195–205°F.**

For light roasts, aim for the higher end: 94–96°C. Light roasts are denser and need more heat to extract fully. Using water that's too cool on a light roast produces a flat, sour, under-extracted cup.

For medium or dark roasts, 90–93°C is sufficient and avoids harsh notes.

If you're bringing water to a boil, let it sit 30 seconds before pouring for a light roast, 45–60 seconds for medium or dark.

## Step-by-Step: Perfect Pour Over

### 1. Boil your water and prepare your filter

Boil more water than you need, since you'll use some to rinse the filter. Fold the filter at the seam and place it in your dripper over your mug or carafe.

### 2. Rinse the filter

Pour hot water through the empty filter until it's saturated. This removes any papery taste and preheats the dripper. Discard the rinse water.

### 3. Add your coffee and tare the scale

Add your ground coffee to the rinsed filter. Shake gently to level the bed. Place everything on the scale and tare to zero.

### 4. Bloom pour (0:00–0:30)

Start your timer. Pour 2–3 times the weight of your coffee in water. For 20g of coffee, pour 40–60g of water. Pour slowly and evenly over all the grounds, making sure everything gets wet.

Let it sit for 30 seconds. This is called the bloom. Fresh coffee releases CO₂ when it first hits hot water, and if you pour straight through, that gas gets in the way of extraction. The bloom lets it escape.

If your coffee blooms aggressively (doming up, lots of bubbling), it's fresh. Less bloom means it's older. Fresh coffee needs a full 30-second bloom; older coffee can go at 20 seconds.

### 5. First pour (0:30–1:00)

Pour slowly in a steady spiral from the center outward, then back inward. Add water until you reach roughly half your total weight. Don't rush. Pour over rewards patience.

Keep the water level consistent. You're not flooding the dripper and waiting for it to drain before pouring again. You're maintaining a steady level with a slow, continuous pour.

### 6. Continue pouring in stages (1:00–2:30)

Continue adding water in slow, steady spirals until you reach your total target weight. You can pour in 2–3 additions or one continuous slow stream. Either works. Just keep pouring slowly.

Avoid pouring directly down the sides of the filter, which can bypass the coffee bed.

### 7. Let it drain and serve

Once you've added all the water, let the dripper drain completely. Remove it. Your total brew time should land between 2:30 and 3:30.

Swirl the carafe once and pour.

## Common Problems and Fixes

**Sour, weak, or flat:** Under-extraction. Grind finer, use hotter water, or slow down your pour.

**Bitter or harsh:** Over-extraction. Grind coarser, use slightly cooler water, or pour faster.

**Uneven draw-down (one side drains before the other):** Your pour is uneven. Try to spiral more consistently, keeping the water level even across the whole bed.

**Coffee tastes papery:** You forgot to rinse the filter. Always rinse first.

**Brew took over 4 minutes:** Grind is too fine. Go coarser.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the pour over coffee ratio?**
The standard ratio is 1:15 coffee to water by weight. For a single cup (250ml), use about 17g of coffee.

**What grind size for pour over?**
Medium-fine, similar to table salt. Finer than drip coffee, coarser than espresso.

**What temperature water for pour over?**
90–96°C (195–205°F). Use the higher end of that range for light roasts, lower end for dark roasts.

**How long should a pour over take?**
Total brew time should be 2:30–3:30 from the first pour to the last drop. Outside that range, adjust your grind.

**Do I need a gooseneck kettle for pour over?**
Technically no, but it helps significantly. The narrow spout gives you slow, precise pour control that a standard kettle can't match.

**What's the difference between V60, Chemex, and Kalita Wave?**
All three produce clean, bright coffee. V60 has the most flow control and is popular with enthusiasts. Chemex uses a thicker filter for an even cleaner cup. Kalita Wave has a flat bed that's more forgiving. For a beginner, Kalita Wave is easiest; for the best cup, V60 or Chemex.
