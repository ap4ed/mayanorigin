## Honduras Has More Than One Great Coffee Region

Most people who know Honduran coffee know two names: Copán and Marcala. Both deserve their reputation. Copán's highland farms produce some of the most complex arabica in Central America. Marcala was the first Honduran coffee to receive a denomination of origin.

But Honduras has six officially recognized coffee regions, and the conversation rarely gets past two of them.

Siguatepeque is one of the ones that gets overlooked. That's a mistake.

## Where Siguatepeque Is

Siguatepeque sits in the Comayagua department, roughly at the geographic center of Honduras. The city itself sits at about 1,100 meters above sea level — already high by most standards — and the farms surrounding it climb considerably higher. Finca El Zapote, the farm we source from, is cultivated at 1,550 masl.

Hondurans call Siguatepeque "the city of eternal spring." The climate stays mild and consistent year-round: cool nights, moderate days, low humidity. For coffee, this translates to a slow, even cherry development that builds flavor complexity without stressing the plant.

## What 1,550 Meters Does to a Coffee Bean

Altitude is the single most important variable in coffee quality — more than variety, more than processing, more than anything else a producer can control.

At 1,550 meters, coffee cherries take weeks longer to ripen than they would at lower elevations. That extended development time allows the sugars inside the cherry to build slowly, which produces a denser bean with more pronounced flavor.

The result, in sensory terms: cleaner acidity, more layered sweetness, and a finish that lingers rather than cutting off flat. It's the difference between a coffee that tastes like it has something to say and one that's just hot and dark.

Copán's farms sit between 1,300 and 1,700 masl. Siguatepeque's best farms, including Finca El Zapote, sit right in the middle of that range — which is why the flavor profile is comparable in quality, even if the names don't carry the same recognition yet.

## Sigua Coffee and Finca El Zapote

Sigua Coffee has been farming and roasting in Honduras since 2013. They operate on a direct-trade model with full traceability back to the farm — you can follow the coffee from the specific plot where it was grown to the bag it ships in.

Finca El Zapote is their flagship single-origin lot. The beans are hand-selected at harvest, which means only fully ripe cherries make it into the bag. Sigua processes the lot using a washed method, which strips the fruit away from the bean before drying. Washed processing emphasizes clarity: the cup reflects the terroir of the farm rather than the character of the fruit.

The farming practices are regenerative — focused on soil health, shade growing, and long-term land stewardship rather than maximizing short-term yield. That philosophy shows in the cup. Well-tended soil produces more complex coffee.
